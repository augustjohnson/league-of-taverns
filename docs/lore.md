# Lore

The story frame for the game, and the few places where it touches the rules.

> **Status: in playtest.** This page describes the direction agreed on
> 2026-10-17: a Survivor-style contest, run as fantasy. The playground plays
> it ([lich-contest-plan.md](lich-contest-plan.md)). [rules.md](rules.md) and
> [decisions.md](decisions.md) still describe the earlier format (gods,
> sponsored trials, parties, loot) until they are rewritten. Where they
> disagree on story, this page wins.

## The premise

**The Lich is looking for a new assistant.** He has gathered about twenty
monsters and told them to decide among themselves who deserves the job.
Every episode he sets them a task. The tasks are contrived and beneath him,
and he finds that part funny. Whoever wins a task is safe for that round.
Then the monsters vote one of their own out.

Voted-out monsters don't leave. The Lich raises them as skeletal staff, and
at the end **the dead choose his assistant**.

## The taverns

The taverns are **fronts for the Lich's organization**: mostly legitimate
businesses, franchises of a company nobody asks too many questions about.
The people in them are not villains. They are ordinary people, usually
drunk: barkeeps, regulars, the odd travelling tinker. They like villains the
way people like true crime, and they enjoy chaos as long as it happens to
somebody else.

They care who wins because **the new assistant becomes their new
representative to the organization**: their boss's boss. Each tavern has
already put favours behind a few candidates, and each one imagines a
different future depending on who gets the job.

They watch it all through the **scrying ball** behind the bar, and argue
about it in the **Common Room**.

## How the story maps to the game

| Story | Game |
|---|---|
| A tavern, one franchise of the Lich's front company | A manager's team. Its name is the team name. |
| The candidates a tavern has put favours behind | The manager's drafted monsters, from a snake draft before the season |
| The Lich's task for the episode | A challenge: a series of checks, set and kept secret before the season, with one winner |
| Winning the task | Immunity for that vote |
| "Decide amongst yourselves" | The vote. Each monster follows the voting strategy on its card |
| Bar gossip about who goes next | Calling the boot: each manager predicts who is voted out, once per vote |
| Voted-out monsters raised as skeletal staff | The jury, which votes for the winner at the end |
| The Lich's new assistant | The season's winner |
| Two taverns competing for the new assistant's favour | A head-to-head matchup in a round-robin schedule |
| The scrying ball | The narrated run of each episode |
| The Common Room | The feed where episodes, votes and calls are told |

Managers **only watch**. Nothing a tavern does reaches the Lich's
contest. The draft and the calls are the decisions.

## Where story touches play

Chrome (names, backstories, story text) still never affects play. That
includes the two story pieces that matter most here:

- **The Lich's tasks.** A task's story ("fetch my phylactery from the
  moat") is chrome. The checks under it (Athletics, a swim speed) are the
  rules.
- **The imagined futures.** After each boot, a tavern's regulars revise
  their daydream about life under each candidate who is still in the running
  ("if the goblin gets it, happy hour every night"; "if the dragon wins,
  we're paying protection in gold"). It changes the conversation, never the
  score. It should follow who is still in the running, not only a tavern's
  own candidates, so that every boot changes the talk at every table.

A monster's **voting strategy** reads like story ("I want a goblinoid to
win", "I'll side with whoever's strongest") but it is a rule. It belongs
with the monster's mechanical data, not its chrome, and should come from a
fixed list, like modifiers do. Creature type, subtype and alignment from the
stat block are natural inputs to it.

The test for adding a story-shaped rule stays the same: **it treats every
tavern the same over a season.**

## Tone

Mundane people next to monstrous things, and nobody too bothered by it.
Terry Pratchett's Ankh-Morpork, where crime runs through respectable guilds,
is the closest match. *What We Do in the Shadows* is good for undead
bureaucracy. The Lich is a bored host, not a threat to the people watching.

## Open questions

- Is the Lich a named character, or just "the Lich"?
- What does the assistant job actually involve?
- Who are the taverns' regulars? Recurring named characters, or a crowd?
- Do any taverns know about each other's ties to the organization?
- What happens to the skeletal staff after the season?
- Do the old terms survive? "League" doesn't fit a Survivor contest, and
  "tavern" now means the team, not the party.
